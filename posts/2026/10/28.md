# Trimension NCJ29D5: Automotive UWB for Digital Keys and Cabin-Aware Experiences

## Overview

An automotive digital key must answer more than “Is this credential valid?” It must also determine whether the authorized device is outside the driver’s door, near the trunk, or inside the vehicle. That distinction separates convenient passive entry from an unsafe authorization decision.

NXP’s **Trimension NCJ29D5** is an automotive ultra-wideband (UWB) IC aimed at secure, precise ranging applications such as digital key. UWB adds a physical-distance measurement to the authentication mechanisms that establish who may access the vehicle.

For in-cabin applications, an important distinction applies: **locating a UWB-equipped phone is not the same as detecting an untagged passenger through radar**. The NCJ29D5 is a relevant starting point for cabin-aware device localization, but its digital-key role should not be interpreted as a guarantee of occupant detection, breathing measurement, or child-presence detection. For those functions, verify the exact device and software offering with NXP.

## Key features

- **Automotive UWB ranging:** Designed for vehicle applications where distance and location help determine access permissions.
- **Secure-ranging foundation:** Associated with IEEE 802.15.4z secure UWB ranging, which strengthens the physical-layer measurement against manipulation.
- **Digital-key relevance:** Supports the UWB portion of a broader vehicle-access architecture; credential management, discovery, and access policy remain system responsibilities.
- **Multi-anchor localization:** Several vehicle-mounted UWB nodes can provide measurements for estimating a device’s position relative to doors and cabin boundaries.
- **Short-pulse, wideband operation:** Enables fine time-of-flight measurements and helps distinguish propagation paths, although vehicle structures still create challenging reflections and obstructions.
- **Integration-dependent performance:** Supported channels, host interfaces, power modes, temperature limits, qualification details, and available firmware features depend on the exact ordering option and software package—check the datasheet.

## Under the hood

UWB ranging measures signal propagation time rather than treating received signal strength as a reliable distance estimate. In two-way ranging, devices exchange timestamped messages and use the timing information to estimate flight time. Because radio waves travel roughly 30 centimeters in one nanosecond, small timing errors can become meaningful position errors.

That makes antenna and RF-path calibration critical. PCB routing, antenna group delay, enclosure materials, and mounting location all influence the final measurement. A development board performing well on a bench does not prove that the same design will perform well behind a door trim panel.

Vehicle bodies also produce substantial multipath. A receiver may observe reflections from metal structures alongside a weak or blocked direct path. UWB provides useful temporal resolution, but non-line-of-sight conditions can still bias ranges. Localization software should evaluate measurement quality, reject inconsistent observations, and avoid turning one questionable range into an unlock decision.

IEEE 802.15.4z introduces mechanisms including the scrambled timestamp sequence, or STS, to support secure ranging. However, **secure ranging is not a complete digital-key security architecture**. Keys must be provisioned and protected, sessions authenticated, and measurements bound to the correct session. The vehicle must also enforce appropriate freshness and authorization rules.

In a typical digital-key system, Bluetooth Low Energy helps with discovery and session coordination, while UWB supplies proximity evidence. NFC can provide a close-range access path where supported. These are separate system functions, not a claim that NCJ29D5 integrates every radio.

## Where it shines (use cases)

### Passive entry and start

Exterior anchors help distinguish a device approaching a door from one farther away. Interior coverage supports a different decision: whether the authorized device is inside the vehicle before enabling start.

The difficult cases are near boundaries: a phone against a window, in a rear pocket beside a door, or inside a bag near the trunk. Anchor placement and decision hysteresis matter more here than a best-case ranging demonstration.

### Cabin-aware device localization

Locating an authorized phone can support driver-side personalization or other cabin-aware behavior. However, a phone’s location does not prove the owner occupies a particular seat. Treat device localization as one input, not a substitute for occupant classification.

### In-cabin sensing evaluation

If “sensing” means detecting people without a participating UWB device, the engineering problem changes. It requires suitable radar measurements, processing, coverage, and validation.

Do not assume a digital-key ranging IC exposes the measurements or firmware needed for that task. Ask NXP explicitly whether NCJ29D5 supports the proposed sensing mode; otherwise, evaluate a dedicated sensing-capable platform.

## Getting started

Start with NXP’s NCJ29D5 documentation and automotive UWB support channel. Confirm access to an **NCJ29D5-based evaluation platform**, compatible firmware, host software, and antenna-reference guidance. Automotive evaluation materials may require additional access arrangements; check current availability rather than assuming a general-purpose development kit is interchangeable.

For a companion host MCU, **MCUXpresso** may be useful when that MCU is supported. Do not assume the public MCUXpresso SDK contains the complete NCJ29D5 automotive digital-key stack. Confirm the supported host, driver package, toolchain, and licensing with NXP.

Build the first prototype around measurable questions:

1. Log ranges, quality indicators, timing, and failed exchanges.
2. Calibrate antenna delays using the intended hardware.
3. Test several anchor locations in the actual vehicle.
4. Exercise phones in pockets, bags, and difficult orientations.
5. Measure false inside/outside decisions, not just average range error.
6. Verify applicable radio regulations and digital-key interoperability requirements.

## Takeaway

NCJ29D5 is best approached as a secure automotive ranging component within a larger digital-key system. Its value comes from combining authenticated sessions, calibrated RF hardware, sensible anchor geometry, and robust access policy.

For cabin applications, define the target carefully: **device localization and tag-free occupant sensing are different capabilities**. Establishing that distinction early prevents an expensive mismatch between the selected IC and the promised feature.
